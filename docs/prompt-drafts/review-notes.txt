TRIAL-SPACE PROMPT REWRITE - REVIEW DRAFT

These files are proposed replacements for the active primer and closing question.
They are outside the package prompt directory and are not used by extraction.
The user's latest clarification remains the committed active prompt in both repos.
This draft has not been evaluated on the LLM.

Design changes:
- Define each field positively before giving a short, explicit omission list.
- Preserve resectability and treatment setting as population-defining information.
- Preserve completed therapy in prior treatment and future treatment context in burden.
- Treat all nine fields as conjunctive. Permit OR consolidation only when it does
  not turn independent eligibility alternatives into simultaneous requirements.
- Specify the meaning of excluded-field lists instead of relying on the heading
  to supply an implicit negation.
- Separate molecular results from specimen logistics and pathological risk features.
- Retain exhaustive diagnosis enumeration for broad cancer trials, separate
  histologies, shared criteria, source exceptions, and the existing output schema.
- Replace repeated warnings and reminders with one brief closing check.

Intentional proposal for review:
The draft removes strict mutual exclusivity. Eligible subgroups can overlap, and
the requested generic basket spaces necessarily overlap the disease-specific
spaces. Forcing a disjoint partition can require unsupported negative criteria
or additional fragmentation. The draft instead removes duplicates and preserves
the source's actual alternatives. This differs from the latest committed wording.

Schema tradeoff:
The nine-field format implies AND across fields. Oncotype OR PAM50 can remain in
one biomarker field. A molecular-score pathway OR a pathological-risk pathway
may still require separate spaces when the alternatives belong in different
fields. Fewer spaces is not a valid improvement if it changes the eligibility set.

Suggested evaluation after review:
Compare this draft with a fixed baseline on the same saved trials, including the
breast and resectable head-and-neck examples, with repeated generations under the
same model and sampling settings. Assess completeness, field placement, logical
equivalence, and appropriate granularity separately. Do not judge only by count.
