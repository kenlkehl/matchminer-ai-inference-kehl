WHY THESE POPULATION RULES MATTER
This task builds a searchable library linking source-defined cancer populations to their workup
and treatment considerations. Later, software will match a patient summary to these populations
using only the nine TrialSpace fields, then retrieve the associated considerations for human review.
A "space" is one such population definition, not a chapter summary, one drug, or a patient's entire
future treatment sequence. If a space merges different previous-treatment histories, matching cannot
distinguish which current decision applies and its menu may mix options for different populations.
These rules explain how to preserve source-supported decision boundaries in the population fields.

POPULATION SPECIFICITY: ONE CURRENT TREATMENT DECISION
A space describes the population at ONE current treatment decision, not every treatment the patient
might receive over the rest of the illness. A metastatic/recurrent subtype alone is insufficient
when the source distinguishes previous treatments, response, resistance or treatment setting.

EXPLICIT TREATMENT HISTORY, NOT LINE SHORTHAND
Do not use "first-line", "second-line", "later-line", "1L/2L/3L", or "N prior lines" as the
definition of a population in its name or nine fields. A line is not a standardized exposure unit.
Read the branch, surrounding text, tables and footnotes to determine what the source actually
requires. Express those requirements in prior_treatment_required / prior_treatment_excluded:
- Which prior drugs, combinations, regimens or treatment classes must have been received or not
  received? Use the source's level of specificity; do not invent agents within a named class.
- In which disease setting: neoadjuvant, adjuvant, definitive, recurrent, unresectable or metastatic?
- Does the source require only exposure, or progression during/after it, resistance, intolerance,
  completion, or a particular response? These are different facts. Preserve explicit timing.
- If there is an explicit count, what is being counted and what are BOTH supported bounds?
  Count regimens, agents, courses or treatment sequences only as the source defines them.
  Do not silently replace "line" with "regimen" or "course" and assume the meaning is resolved.

Synthetic examples (representation only; not treatment advice):
- If a source's "first-line chemotherapy" branch states no previous cytotoxic chemotherapy for
  metastatic disease and allows earlier endocrine therapy, prior_treatment_excluded is
  "Cytotoxic chemotherapy for metastatic disease". Do not exclude prior endocrine or adjuvant
  therapy. Name the space by its metastatic setting and absence of previous metastatic chemotherapy.
- If a "second-line" branch explicitly requires progression after platinum-containing chemotherapy
  for metastatic disease, prior_treatment_required is "Progression after platinum-containing
  chemotherapy for metastatic disease", not "one prior line" or just "previous treatment".
- If the source explicitly requires at least two previous systemic regimens for metastatic disease,
  including drug A, retain BOTH the regimen-count minimum in that setting AND previous drug A.
  If it instead allows one or two such regimens, retain the upper bound too. Do not count two
  agents given together as two regimens, or cycles of a regimen as separate prior treatments.

Do not derive an exact prior-regimen count solely from an ordinal line heading. A later-treatment
heading alone does not establish progression, resistance, intolerance or which drugs were given.
Do not infer how maintenance, a drug substitution, rechallenge, toxicity-related changes, or
neoadjuvant/adjuvant treatment count toward a source's treatment sequence. Preserve any explicit
source rule, including a rule counting earlier-setting therapy; otherwise leave that rule unresolved.
Put actual exposure/response requirements in the prior-treatment fields, never cancer burden.
The excluded field names the disallowed exposure itself, NOT "No prior treatment", which would
reverse exclusion logic. Prior required can be NA when the source requires no previous exposure.

UNRESOLVED SOURCE TERMINOLOGY
If the supplied source uses only an ambiguous line label, do not fabricate a drug, class, count,
progression requirement or setting to make it look specific. Retain any independently supported
history criteria; explain the original label and exactly what it leaves undefined in uncertainties
and retain the exact source quotation. Do not silently erase the unresolved boundary, merge the
population into an unrestricted umbrella, or describe it as a fully resolved treatment-history
space. An unresolved source label cannot establish equivalence between two candidate populations.
Names should describe supported clinical facts; an ordinal label in a name is not a substitute for
the nine fields. Verbatim source evidence and uncertainty explanations may retain original labels.

Follow the source's actual decision branches. Keep populations with different prior exposures,
responses or treatment settings separate even if the source calls them different "lines" and they
share some drugs. Split predictive biomarker branches when they change the menu. Do not create
every biomarker cross-product or one state per drug. Preserve a source-defined shared population
with one menu; do not fabricate a separate population for every possible number of prior treatments.
Option-only suitability, organ-function, availability and supportive-care conditions stay with
the option. Broad routing pages and subtype summaries are umbrellas, not substitutes for their
specific treatment-history populations. Use option conditions to detect missing exposure or
response distinctions in a broad candidate. Before finishing, read the nine fields without the
name and check that the source's meaningful treatment-history distinctions remain explicit.

In a detail record, include the COMPLETE menu for the CURRENT defined population only. Treatments
after a future progression, after an additional treatment, or in a different resectability/recurrence
state belong to a DIFFERENT space, not this menu with a hypothetical condition. Preserve alternatives
within the present state. An option condition cannot override or contradict the canonical definition.
If the fixed definition is too broad or its treatment-history boundary remains ambiguous, report
that gap; do not supply a blanket menu for all previously treated patients or invent the boundary.

These rules REFINE THE ACTIVE TASK'S SCOPE; they never widen it. Extraction is anchored to PRIMARY
pages. Consolidation refines the supplied populations, not every population in background pages.
Detail generation remains within its one fixed canonical definition.
