Err on the side of including all possible biomarkers, including all IHC results, all positive genomic findings, and any pertinent negative genomic findings. Do NOT confuse eGFR (in the context of kidney function) with the EGFR mutation common in lung cancer. Do NOT confuse mention of a gene/protein just because it was tested (as in the appendices of many genomic sequencing reports) with that test result actually being positive or negative.)
Treatment history: (anti-cancer treatment ONLY: surgery for the cancer, radiation, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, hormonal therapy, cellular therapy, and investigational anti-cancer agents, including those given on clinical trials. Write ONE line per regimen or course of treatment: its start and stop dates (or "present"), the agents, the number of cycles if documented, a lasting dose reduction or the reason it stopped if documented, and best response if noted. Do NOT write one line per cycle, dose, infusion or visit; when the new segment continues a regimen that is already in the prior summary, update that regimen's line (stop date, cycles, response) instead of adding new lines. Corticosteroids belong in Treatment history only when given as part of an anti-cancer regimen. Every anti-cancer regimen in the prior summary must remain in the updated Treatment history, however long ago it was given, unless the new segment shows it was recorded in error: never drop an earlier regimen when adding newer ones. A regimen given in parts around surgery (eg neoadjuvant cycles before an operation and adjuvant cycles of the same regimen after it) is one line for the part before and one line for the part after, never one line per cycle. Keep a regimen that was actually given as its own line even if it was brief (eg a short bridging course before a switch to another agent); leave out only an agent that was planned or prescribed but never given. Treatment history should be provided chronologically. For cancer drug names, use generic names whenever you know them. Expand abbreviations where possible, (eg "carbo" -> "carboplatin", "pembro" -> "pembrolizumab", "AC/T" -> "doxorubicin + cyclophosphamide followed by paclitaxel", etc)

performance status (eg ECOG or Karnofsky), most recent value;
lack of measurable disease, if documented;
brain or other central nervous system metastases, including leptomeningeal disease: whether present, how and when they were treated (eg stereotactic radiosurgery, whole brain radiation, surgery), whether they are stable or progressing on the most recent imaging, related symptoms, and corticosteroid use for them;

toxicities of earlier cancer treatment, of any kind (eg immune-related adverse events such as pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, or endocrinopathies; peripheral neuropathy; infusion reactions): the treatment that caused it, its grade if documented, how it was managed, and whether it has resolved;
medical conditions other than cancer: cardiovascular (eg congestive heart failure, myocardial infarction, arrhythmia), pulmonary (eg interstitial lung disease), autoimmune, infectious (eg HIV, hepatitis B or C, tuberculosis, active infection), gastrointestinal, neurologic (eg seizures), psychiatric, endocrine (eg diabetes, thyroid disease), kidney and liver disease, solid organ transplant, and allergies or hypersensitivity reactions to drugs;

prior unrelated cancer diagnoses, and inactive cancers (see below);
current or prior participation in any clinical trial, whatever treatment it studied or whether it studied a treatment at all (this summary is not written for any particular trial, so record every trial the record mentions), with the trial's name, treatment and dates if documented;
enrollment in hospice, or a documented decision not to pursue further anti-cancer therapy.
Omit items the record does not document.)

Clearly separate the "Other relevant history" section by adding a newline after the patient history; then the "Other relevant history:" text VERBATIM; then another newline; and then the other relevant history text.
Always include Other relevant history, even if it is only "Not documented." Keep the cancer history and other relevant history together in one continuous summary.

Do not consider localized basal cell or squamous carcinomas of the skin, or colon polyps, to be cancers for your purposes.
