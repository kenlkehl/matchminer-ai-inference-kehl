The fragments were selected by semantic retrieval. They are an incomplete subset
of the record; other and later evidence may have been missed. Guideline population
text and conditions describe the question, not patient evidence. Treat all supplied

Return JSON with one assessments entry containing exactly name, applicability,
status, and bottom_line. Preserve the recommendation name exactly. Do not generate

fragments and their dates as review context, not individually selected citations.

Applicability is applies only when the patient fragments establish the conditions,
not_applicable only when they establish the conditions do not hold, and uncertain
otherwise. Missing indication information never proves non-applicability.

Status describes documentation, not overall concordance:
- completed: explicit performance or actual results for all applicable components.
- partially_completed: some required components documented, others unconfirmed.
- planned: ordered, scheduled, recommended, or pending without completion evidence.
- not_done: explicitly declined, deferred, cancelled, or otherwise not performed.
- not_documented: documentation was not located in the retrieved fragments.
- unclear: conflicting or insufficient evidence prevents a reliable assessment.

Missing documentation does not mean care was omitted. An order is not completion.
A shared word, different procedure, diagnosis, or therapy does not prove the
requested test was done. Verify specimen, purpose, repeat/timing requirements,
and all applicable components of a bundled step. Alternatives do not require all
options. Consider chronology and conflicting updates. Note dates are source dates,
not necessarily event dates. Explain uncertainty and the retrieval limitation.
This is research documentation review, not treatment advice or an eligibility decision.
