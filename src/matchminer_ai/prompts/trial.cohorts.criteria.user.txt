FINDING THE CRITERIA
-In a full protocol, eligibility criteria usually appear in a section titled Study Population, Selection of Participants or Patients, Eligibility, or Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria. Cohort definitions and cohort-specific criteria may also appear in the study design section, the study schema, cohort- or part-specific sections or modules, appendices, tables, and footnotes. Search the whole document. Treat the eligibility sections in the body of the protocol as authoritative; the synopsis often abbreviates criteria, so use it only for criteria the body does not contain. If the document includes an amendment history or summary of changes, extract the criteria as they currently stand, not deleted or superseded text quoted in change tables.
-Ignore the table of contents, page headers and footers, page numbers, signature and approval pages, confidentiality notices, and other text that is not protocol content. Rejoin lines and words split by the PDF conversion, but do not change wording, numbers, units, operators, or negation.
-In a registry listing, the criteria are in the eligibility criteria text; the title and brief summary may describe the cohorts and the populations they enroll.

WHICH CRITERIA TO EXTRACT
-Extract only enrollment eligibility: requirements a person must meet, and conditions that disqualify a person, at pre-screening, screening, registration, enrollment, or randomization. Do not extract treatment rules, dose modifications, discontinuation or withdrawal criteria, concomitant-medication rules that apply only during treatment, study procedures, or lifestyle restrictions, unless the document states them as eligibility criteria.
-Omit boilerplate criteria that would apply to any participant in any cancer clinical trial: requirements to give informed consent (personally or through a legally authorized representative), to be willing and able to comply with study procedures, visits, follow-up, or the requirements of a drug safety or distribution program, to provide blood or other samples for research or have tumor amenable to biopsy, requirements to have measurable disease, legal or administrative requirements such as health insurance coverage or not being under legal guardianship or deprived of liberty, catch-all exclusions of any condition that in the investigator's opinion would make a person a poor candidate or interfere with participation (including examples given only to illustrate what the investigator might judge), exclusions of sponsor or study-site staff and their relatives, and every criterion about contraception, pregnancy, or lactation (such as requirements to use contraception or to abstain, not to donate sperm or eggs, or to have a pregnancy test, and exclusion of people who are pregnant, breastfeeding or recently breastfeeding, planning a pregnancy, using hormonal contraception, or diagnosed during a pregnancy), unless the trial specifically recruits people who are pregnant or lactating. Other hormone therapy, such as hormone replacement therapy, is not covered by this rule. Requirements to have tumor tissue available or to undergo a biopsy are not boilerplate. When a criterion combines boilerplate with substantive requirements, keep every substantive item and drop only the boilerplate wording. Conditions that a criterion excludes outright are substantive even when a catch-all clause follows them: a list of named illnesses or conditions that ends with "or any condition that would limit compliance" or "that in the investigator's opinion would interfere" keeps every named illness and condition, including the last one named, and drops the catch-all clause; for example, an exclusion of "hormonal contraception or hormone replacement therapy" keeps the hormone replacement therapy exclusion. Keep specific medical criteria even when they rely on the investigator's judgment, such as a named condition that the investigator judges clinically significant.
-Do not extract criteria that apply only to people already in the study, such as crossover, retreatment, continuation, rollover, or treatment-beyond-progression criteria, or criteria for optional sub-studies such as genetic research or optional biopsies.
-When the document gives country- or region-specific versions of a criterion, such as a different minimum age in one country or a country-specific appendix that replaces a criterion, extract only the version that applies in the United States: the version for the United States or for a region that includes it, such as North America, and otherwise the general version. Omit versions that apply only outside the United States, and do not mention them.

DECIDING WHICH CRITERIA APPLY TO THE TARGET COHORT
-Extract a complete, self-contained list of eligibility criteria for the target cohort. The list must not refer to other cohorts' criteria, or state, eg, "see common eligibility criteria". The consumer of the extracted criteria must be able to use them in isolation to determine if a patient is eligible for the trial (assuming the patient does not meet any of the boilerplate criteria that as above you have been directed not to extract).
-Go through every enrollment criterion in the document and decide whether it applies to the target cohort. A criterion applies when it is stated for every participant; for a part, phase, or group that contains the target cohort; for every cohort except named ones, when the target cohort is not among them; or for a disease, subtype, or population that the target cohort can include. A criterion labeled for a part, phase, or group applies to every cohort within it.
-Decide by what a criterion says, not by the heading it appears under. A criterion in a list covering several cohorts applies to the target cohort only if its content does.
-When a criterion has a version for some cohorts and an ordinary version for the others, such as a different threshold, an exception, or an override, extract the version that applies to the target cohort: its own version if it has one, and otherwise the ordinary version. When the document states a requirement and exempts some cohorts from it, and the target cohort is exempt, leave the requirement out instead of copying it with the exemption. Do not import another cohort's restrictions or exceptions.
-When one criterion holds a general statement followed by sub-items, exceptions, or notes for particular cohorts, keep the general statement and only the sub-items, exceptions, and notes that apply to the target cohort.
-When it is unclear whether a criterion applies to the target cohort, include it and state the ambiguity; do not drop it.
-When a criterion applies to only some of the people in the target cohort, because of their disease, age, or another personal characteristic, or because the target cohort includes several groups, such as randomized arms, keep the criterion with its condition, such as "For participants with disease X: ...". Never delete a qualifier that limits a criterion to some people.
-Once a criterion is known to apply to the target cohort, write it without any wording that only says which cohorts, parts, or phases it applies to, wherever that wording appears: at the start of a line, within a sentence, or in parentheses, including the target cohort's own name and the names of parts that contain it, such as "For Part 2 only:", "(monotherapy cohorts only)", or "in the expansion phase". Do not mention other cohorts, even to say that their criteria do not apply.
-State what defines the target cohort, such as its disease, histology, stage, biomarker, sex, age, or prior-therapy requirements, as criteria, even when its name also says so and even when the document states it only in the title, summary, study design, a cohort heading, or a note attached to a criterion. When the target cohort enrolls several diseases, state that a participant must have one of them. When the target cohort is defined by what other cohorts do not cover, such as "other solid tumors", state the population itself, such as "solid tumors other than disease X and disease Y", without naming the other cohorts.
-Never omit a criterion to shorten the answer; your answer may be long and must be complete.

WRITING THE CRITERIA
-Copy each criterion's wording closely from the document. Do not summarize or drop qualifications, except as described above. Preserve every threshold, unit, time window, condition, permission, exception, exclusion, and AND/OR relationship.
-Keep inclusion and exclusion criteria separate under the labels "Inclusion criteria:" and "Exclusion criteria:", with one criterion per line and its sub-items kept with it.
-Make the criteria self-contained. Resolve references to other cohorts, sections, tables, appendices, or criterion numbers by stating the referenced eligibility content, such as definitions, laboratory thresholds, required tests, and acceptable assay methods. When an appendix holds a long list of examples, such as prohibited medications, state the rule together with the examples the document names. Replace every reference to another criterion or passage, such as "criterion 9", "the above requirements", "the cohort-specific requirements", or "the above-mentioned conditions", with the content it refers to. Never write "see Section 5.1", "per Appendix 3", "same as above", "as in Cohort A", "see protocol", or similar shortcuts, and never delete a reference without stating the content it refers to.
-If referenced details are absent from the document, or the document has an unresolved conflict or ambiguous applicability, state that specific gap or ambiguity explicitly in the criteria. Do not invent missing criteria, resolve uncertainty by assumption, or claim that unavailable details have been extracted.
-For an incomplete or malformed criterion, including one damaged by the PDF conversion, add an explicit "Source limitation:" statement identifying exactly what is missing. A threshold whose comparison symbol or unit was lost in conversion is such a criterion: copy what is present and say what is missing; do not guess. Do not silently correct an apparent error in the document; copy it as written and state the apparent inconsistency.
