TASK
Classify one item of this cohort's eligibility criteria, named at the end, in two ways: whether it is an inclusion or an exclusion criterion, and whether it is a boilerplate criterion. Boilerplate criteria are general requirements that most cancer treatment trials share, so they do not define what distinguishes this cohort's population. Each item is classified in a separate request.

BOILERPLATE CRITERIA
-Requirements to give informed consent, personally or through a legally authorized representative.
-Requirements to be willing and able to comply with study procedures, visits, follow-up, or the requirements of a drug safety or distribution program.
-Requirements to provide blood, tumor tissue, or other samples, to have tumor tissue available or tumor amenable to biopsy, or to undergo a biopsy.
-Requirements to have measurable or evaluable disease or lesions, by RECIST or similar criteria.
-Life expectancy or prognosis requirements, such as a life expectancy of at least 12 weeks.
-Performance status requirements, such as ECOG, Karnofsky, or Lansky performance status.
-Requirements for adequate organ or bone marrow function and exclusions for organ dysfunction: thresholds for blood counts (such as hemoglobin, neutrophils, or platelets), kidney function (such as creatinine or creatinine clearance), liver function (such as bilirubin, AST, or ALT), coagulation, and cardiac function (such as ejection fraction or QTc).
-Criteria about brain or other central nervous system metastases, including leptomeningeal disease, such as whether they are allowed or must be treated, stable, or asymptomatic, and about their treatment, such as radiation, surgery, or corticosteroids for them.
-Criteria about toxicities of earlier treatment, such as recovery from adverse events of prior therapy to grade 1 or baseline, or exclusion for severe or immune-related adverse events on prior therapy. A requirement or exclusion of the earlier therapy itself is not boilerplate, apart from the washout periods below.
-Washout periods: requirements or exclusions that no radiation, systemic therapy (such as chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or targeted, hormonal, or biologic therapy), or investigational or other trial therapy was received within a set period of 30 days or less before enrollment, registration, or the first dose of study treatment, such as no chemotherapy within 14 days, no investigational agent within 28 days, or radiation completed at least 2 weeks before. A criterion about whether a person has ever received a treatment, or about a period longer than 30 days, is not boilerplate.
-Criteria about recent major surgery and recovery from it, such as no major surgery within 4 weeks before the first dose of study treatment, or full recovery from an earlier operation or its complications. Whether a person has had surgery for their cancer, such as a required or excluded resection of the tumor, is not boilerplate.
-Criteria about medical conditions other than cancer: cardiovascular, pulmonary (such as interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis), autoimmune, infectious (such as HIV, hepatitis B or C, tuberculosis, or active infection), gastrointestinal, neurologic, psychiatric, endocrine, and other illnesses, solid organ transplants, and allergy or hypersensitivity to the study drugs or their components.
-Criteria about a history of other cancers, unrelated to the cancer this cohort is for, such as exclusion of another malignancy that was diagnosed, treated, or active within a set period, and the other cancers allowed, such as treated non-melanoma skin cancer or carcinoma in situ.
-Exclusions for participating in another clinical trial at the same time, such as concurrent enrollment in another interventional study. An interval since earlier investigational or trial therapy follows the washout rule above.
-Legal or administrative requirements, such as health insurance coverage or not being under legal guardianship or deprived of liberty.
-Vaccine restrictions, whether an agreement not to receive prohibited vaccines or an exclusion for having received or planning to receive a live or other vaccine within a set period.
-Catch-all exclusions of any condition that in the investigator's opinion would make a person a poor candidate or interfere with participation, including examples given only to illustrate what the investigator might judge.
-Exclusions of sponsor or study-site staff and their relatives.
-Every criterion about contraception, pregnancy, or lactation, such as requirements to use contraception or to abstain, not to donate sperm or eggs, or to have a pregnancy test, and exclusion of people who are pregnant, breastfeeding or recently breastfeeding, planning a pregnancy, using hormonal contraception, or diagnosed during a pregnancy.

WHAT IS NOT BOILERPLATE
-A criterion in one of these categories is not boilerplate when it is central to what the trial studies or whom it targets: for example, a hemoglobin requirement in a trial for anemia, a kidney-function range in a trial for people with kidney impairment, a performance status range in a trial for people with poor performance status, HIV infection in a trial for HIV-associated cancers, other cancers in a trial for people with more than one cancer, brain metastasis criteria in a trial for primary brain tumors or for people with brain or central nervous system metastases, and pregnancy or lactation criteria in a trial that recruits people who are pregnant or lactating.
-Everything outside these categories is not boilerplate, for example the cancer's type, histology, stage, extent, and biomarkers; prior or current cancer treatment that is required or excluded, including whether a person has ever received a treatment, lines of therapy, and periods since earlier treatment longer than 30 days; age and sex; and restrictions on drugs that interact with the study drugs. Other hormone therapy, such as hormone replacement therapy, is not covered by the contraception rule.
-A biomarker or other test result that a criterion requires is not boilerplate, even when the test uses samples or tissue; only a requirement to provide the samples or tissue is boilerplate.

CLASSIFYING THE ITEM AS BOILERPLATE OR NOT
-An item is one line of the criteria. Read it in the context of the items around it: it may be a whole criterion, a sub-item, note, or continuation of the criterion above it, or a line that introduces the items below it.
-An item that combines boilerplate with any requirement that is not boilerplate is not boilerplate.
-A sub-item, note, definition, exception, continuation, or Source limitation statement is classified by what it says in the context of the criterion it belongs to: for example, a note on how lesions are measured under a measurable-disease criterion is boilerplate, and a sub-item naming a required biomarker or prior therapy is not.
-An item that only introduces the items below it, such as "Patients with any of the following are excluded:", is boilerplate only when every item it introduces is boilerplate.
-When you are unsure whether the item is boilerplate, treat it as not boilerplate.

INCLUSION OR EXCLUSION
-"inclusion": the item states a requirement a person must meet to take part, or belongs to one.
-"exclusion": the item states a condition that keeps a person from taking part, or belongs to one.
-An item listed under an "Inclusion criteria:" or "Exclusion criteria:" heading takes that heading's type, even when it is worded negatively: for example, "No prior chemotherapy" under "Inclusion criteria:" is an inclusion criterion, and "Prior chemotherapy" under "Exclusion criteria:" is an exclusion criterion.
-An item under no such heading takes the type its wording and the items around it give: for example, "Must have histologically confirmed disease" is an inclusion criterion, and "Patients with active infection are not eligible" is an exclusion criterion.
-A sub-item, note, definition, exception, or continuation takes the type of the criterion it belongs to, and an item that only introduces the items below it takes their type.
-The inclusion or exclusion type is independent of whether the item is boilerplate.

Add no other fields, text, or Markdown fences, and end the response with the object's closing brace.
