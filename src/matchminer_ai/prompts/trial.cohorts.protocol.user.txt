DECIDING WHETHER THIS IS A CANCER TREATMENT TRIAL
-Determine whether the trial actually evaluates a direct anti-cancer treatment strategy. Classify the intervention being studied, not merely the participants' cancer diagnosis or their background therapy.
-Include trials evaluating treatments intended to kill, reduce, or control cancer: anti-cancer drugs, therapeutic biologics, immunotherapy or cellular therapy, therapeutic radiation, cancer surgery or ablation, and combinations or comparisons of these strategies. Include neoadjuvant, adjuvant, maintenance, and prevention of recurrence after treatment of an established cancer; measurable or currently active disease is not required.
-Anti-cancer treatment studies MUST be included even when their only outcomes are pharmacokinetic (PK), pharmacodynamic (PD), safety, tolerability, dose-finding, feasibility, target engagement, or other biomarker/surrogate endpoints. Clinical efficacy, tumor response, progression, or survival endpoints are NOT required.
-Exclude studies of diagnostic-only imaging, tracers, biopsies, screening, and biomarker-only sampling or monitoring studies that do not evaluate an anti-cancer treatment. Trials of supportive care, symptom management, toxicity prevention, rehabilitation, survivorship, and primary cancer prevention are not cancer treatment trials. A therapeutic radiopharmaceutical can qualify as cancer treatment. Mixed-purpose trials qualify only when they actually evaluate a direct anti-cancer strategy.
-Set cancer_treatment_trial to "yes" only when the protocol establishes that the trial evaluates a direct anti-cancer treatment strategy. Otherwise set it to "no", stop, and do not assess cohorts or extract eligibility criteria. Do not add a rationale or any other field to a "no" response.
