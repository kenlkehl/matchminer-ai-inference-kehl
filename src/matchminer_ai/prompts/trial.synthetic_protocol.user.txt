Requirements:
- Seed, not specification: base the study concept, design, parts, cohorts, arms, interventions and eligible patients on the record. The protocol does not have to match the record exactly. Like a real protocol compared with its registry entry, it may refine, extend or differ in details such as thresholds, line counts or added restrictions, as long as the result is a coherent, plausible protocol for this trial. Do any comparison with the record in your reasoning only. Eligibility details that appear in the record's title, summary or description (for example, which patients are permitted or excluded) belong only in the study population section and synopsis; do not carry them into the background or rationale.
- Timing: write the protocol as approved before enrollment began, in the present and future tense ("participants will receive"). Registry records are often updated later and may be written in the past tense or describe later events such as enrollment halts, terminations, parts that were never started, end-of-trial dates or results. Do not include those events, an enrollment or study status, or anything else that would not be known before the first participant enrolled; describe every planned part of the study as planned.
- Study design: define the study's parts, cohorts, arms and any disease- or biomarker-defined groups in the study design section, with a schema, using names the rest of the document repeats. Outside the synopsis and the study population section, describe who enrolls only at the level of the study design, such as the disease setting of each part or cohort; do not list, preview or justify individual eligibility rules in the background, rationale, design or any other section.
- Eligibility is the most important section. Write it at the level of detail a real protocol of this kind, phase and sponsor would have: industry-sponsored protocols often run 2,000 to 4,000 words of eligibility, while investigator-initiated and smaller studies are often much shorter. Where such a protocol would, include explicit laboratory thresholds with units; acceptable specimen types, assay methods, laboratory accreditation and positivity thresholds for each biomarker; prior-therapy rules (number of prior lines, required or excluded agents, washout periods); central nervous system metastasis rules; and cardiac, infection, organ-function, gastrointestinal, reproductive, radiotherapy and surgery criteria, plus the other standard criteria a protocol for this trial would contain.
- Eligibility layout: {eligibility_layout} Whatever the layout, real protocols do not sort criteria under headings such as "common criteria", "criteria applicable to all cohorts", "cohort-specific criteria" or "population-specific criteria", and do not label groups of criteria as populations; do not do so either. If the study enrolls a single group, use one numbered inclusion list and one numbered exclusion list.
- Texture: real eligibility text is often messily specified, and this document should read that way. Open each list with one short sentence in the sponsor's usual style (for example, "Patients are eligible to be included in the study only if all of the following criteria apply at screening:"), without explaining how lists in different sections combine. Use the sponsor's numbering and nesting habits, notes ("NOTE:") and inline exceptions, abbreviations defined elsewhere in the document, and references to other sections, tables or appendices of this document; every section, table or appendix you refer to must exist in the document. Some judgments may be left to the investigator. Every criterion must still make clinical sense and be decidable from the patient's record and the study's screening procedures. Do not add numbered entries that state no requirement.
- Study-treatment criteria: for every study agent, including any comparator, check its approved label or known toxicity profile for histology or indication limits, renal and hepatic function limits, contraindications, drug interactions and contraception durations after the last dose. Carry each into eligibility, concomitant-medication rules and contraception requirements; where agents differ, apply the strictest requirement to participants who receive them.
- Names: use the drug names and codes that appear in the record. Do not invent additional codes or names for those products.
- Structure: title page (protocol number, version, date, sponsor), protocol synopsis (with only key or abbreviated eligibility criteria, as real synopses give, referring to the study population section for the full list), schedule of activities table, background and rationale, objectives and endpoints table, study design, study population, study treatments with dose-modification and toxicity-management tables, concomitant medications, study assessments and procedures, adverse-event definitions and reporting, statistical considerations (hypotheses, sample size and its assumptions, analysis populations, interim analyses, multiplicity), data management, ethics and regulatory considerations, and appendices.
- Write every section in full prose and tables at the length of a complete sponsor protocol. Do not use placeholders such as "[to be added]" or "TBD", and do not abbreviate with "etc." or "per standard practice" where a protocol would state the rule.
- Consistency: before writing, settle the key parameters (arms, doses, cycle length, visit schedule and windows, washout periods, sample size and statistical assumptions) and use them identically in every section. Check the sample-size and alpha-spending arithmetic. Before finishing, check that no eligibility criterion is reversed elsewhere without an explicit exception.
- The document must never mention the registry record, a source or seed record, or how the protocol or its criteria were derived, and must not include any statement, disclaimer, note or watermark saying it is synthetic, fictional, reconstructed or AI-generated. Write the document as the protocol itself.
- Do not cite specific publications or include a reference list; describe the background evidence in prose.
- These requirements are instructions to you as the author. Do not restate, paraphrase or explain any of them in the document.

Output only the protocol document in Markdown.
