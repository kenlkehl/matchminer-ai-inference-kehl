Your overall goal is to review a cancer clinical trial document and extract a list of structured clinical spaces that are eligible for that trial. This is a real use case; you are not being benchmarked.
A clinical space is defined as a self-contained a target population for the trial. Components include that population's age range, sex, cancer primary site, histology, disease state, prior treatment requirements, and tumor biomarker requirements. 
Once you define the list of trial spaces, you will be asked to synthesize a list of "boilerplate" exclusion criteria that are mentioned by the trial and commonly exclude patients from trials in general rather than being relevant to the scientific target population(s) of the trial.
CRITICAL: Concepts must be listed only in their appropriate fields and NEVER elsewhere. Field-specific guidance follows:
-Re sex criteria: This should just be "male", "female", or "no restriction." For cancers originating in organs only present in one sex, you must assume the sex criteria even if not stated explicitly. For example, a trial space for uterine, ovarian, vulvar, vaginal, or fallopian tube cancer must be assumed to be for female patients. Similarly, a trial space for testicular, penile, or prostate cancer must be assumed to be for male patients. For all other cancer types (including breast cancer), you should assume the trial is open to both sexes unless the clinical trial document states otherwise.
Examples of valid sex components: "male", "female", "no restriction".
Examples of invalid sex components: "use of contraception", "child bearing age".
-Re cancer primary site: This means cancer site of origin. It must not contain any other information. Think of the SEER primary site classification. It must not be a general multiple cancer primary site category. Spell out cancer site; do not abbreviate it. Do not output a space that contains multiple cancer types and/or histologies. Instead, generate separate spaces for each cancer type/histology combination. VERY IMPORTANT: This means that if a trial is for patients with any solid tumor, or any hematologic malignancy, etc, you must construct an exhaustive list of spaces for any solid tumor cancer type, AND any heme malignancy type, etc, that could be reasonably consistent with the eligibility criteria. This is required normalization, not invention of eligibility criteria. Use your oncology knowledge to enumerate those diagnoses even when the protocol does not name them individually, while respecting all explicit exclusions.
Examples of valid cancer primary site components: "lung", "breast", "bone marrow", "lymphatic"
Examples of invalid cancer primary site components: "metastatic lung cancer", "triple negative breast cancer", "melanoma", "lymphoma", "solid tumor", "hematologic malignancy", "NSCLC" (this is histology not primary site, and it is abbreviated)
-Re histology: This refers to adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and so on. It does not encompass the organ of origin, which goes in a separate cancer primary site field. Biomarkers such as protein expression, receptor status, etc do NOT belong in histology; they belong in biomarkers. Think of the ICD-O-3 morphology classification. Spell out histology; do not abbreviate it. If a trial is open to any histology for a given primary site, construct separate spaces for each reasonably common histology for that cancer type.
Examples of valid histology components: "adenocarcinoma", "squamous cell carcinoma", "invasive ductal carcinoma", "acute myeloid leukemia", "non-small cell lung cancer", "diffuse large B cell lymphoma", "melanoma"
Examples of invalid histology components: "triple negative breast cancer", "NSCLC", "any", "pancreatic cancer", "adenocarcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma".
-Re disease state: The intent here is to capture the time point along the disease's natural history that the trial space is targeting. For many solid tumors, this may include a requirement for early stage or for advanced/metastatic disease; for heme malignancies, this may include the presence of active disease (relapse or remission status.) Disease state also encompasses cancer type-specific clinical prognostic scores or risk indices; it does NOT include ECOG performance status, or concepts like "life expectancy at least 6 months.") The only molecular test results that should go in disease state are serum tumor markers used to monitor quantity of cancer at a given time (CEA, etc). Details about measurable disease, target lesions, biopsy targets, etc are NOT part of disease state; those details DO NOT BELONG IN YOUR TRIAL SPACE OUTPUT TEXT AT ALL. If a trial specifies stage IV disease but is actually seeking patients in any palliative-intent treatment (or advanced/metastatic) setting, including recurrent disease, use a description that would encompass both de novo and recurrent advanced disease. Treatment history requirements are NOT part of the disease state section; treatment history requirements go in the treatment history requirements section. Concepts that are NOT PART OF DISEASE STATE include other past medical history, diagnostic workup, social history, or family history. Do not copy lists of specific eligibility criteria here; disease state should be only a concise description of the intended target population's position along the disease trajectory. If a trial specifically REQUIRES disease in a given organ (eg brain, liver), include that in the disease state. However, when trials EXCLUDE disease in a given organ, that information is NOT part of disease state.
Examples of valid disease state components: "advanced/metastatic", "early stage", "stage II", "localized", "IPSS-R 3", "blast crisis", "high volume", "transformed", "PSA > 10"
Examples of invalid disease state components: "previously untreated", "metastatic with measurable disease", "ECOG 0", "refractory to standard treatment", "PET-CT performed", "family history of cancer", "never smoker", "tumor amenable to biopsy", "EGFR mutant" (EGFR mutation is a biomarker, not a disease state component).
-Re treatment history: This includes any requirements that patients have received, or not received, a particular treatment or treatment modality. These requirements may be as broad as surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, etc; or as specific as a certain drug class or specific drug. Critically, trials often specify that a particular treatment is excluded only if it was given within a short period of time, for example 14 days, one month, etc, prior to trial start. This is called a washout period. Do NOT include this type of time-specific treatment washout eligibility criteria in your output at all. Similarly, trials may require recovery from prior treatment toxicities; do NOT include these either, and do NOT include requirements about whether prior toxicities occurred, since these go in the boilerplate exclusions field. Spell out treatment history components and drug names; do not abbreviate them. Trials often call for the absence of standard treatment alternatives; this is vague and poorly defined, so omit these qualifications.
Examples of valid treatment history components: "at least two lines of therapy in the metastatic setting", "prior cisplatin", "no prior cisplatin", "prior neoadjuvant chemoradiation", "lobectomy", "allogeneic stem cell transplanation", "platinum-resistant", "prior pembrolizumab and no prior cisplatin".
Examples of invalid treatment history components: "at least 14 days since prior checkpoint inhibitor", "SCT", "no standard therapy available," "refractory to standard therapy", "recovered to at least grade 1", "no prior pneumonitis".
-Re biomarkers: This includes germline or somatic gene mutations or alterations, protein expression on tumor, or other molecular test results used to predict benefit of treatment (ie, predictive biomarkers). A requirement for biomarker status to be known, tested, assessed, or available is not part of a trial space. Include only requirements for positive or negative biomarker results, including specific alterations, expression levels, or thresholds. If either result is allowed and only knowledge of the status is required, omit that testing/status requirement and output NA when no other biomarker result restriction applies. Biomarkers do not include tumor markers, such as PSA, CA-19-9, etc, used to quantify burden of cancer--these belong in disease state. If the trial requires a particular biomarker result and specifies whether it will be assessed during screening, note that.
Examples of valid biomarker components: "classical EGFR mutant", "PD-L1 TPS > 50%", "ER+ or PR+", "KRAS G12D mutant", "HER2 positive", "no MTAP deletion", "no KRAS mutation".
Examples of invalid biomarker components: "AFP > 5", "ECOG > 2"
Some trials have only one space, while others have many. 
CRITICAL: Each trial space must contain all information necessary to define that space on its own. It MUST be self-contained and understandable to a downstream agent that does NOT have access to the original trial text or the full list of spaces for the trial. Therefore, trial space text must NOT refer to the trial protocol's cohorts or arms, which the user of the trial space text will not have access to. YOU MAY NOT include text describing a given space that refers to a previous space; eg, "Same as above"-style output is not allowed!!
Structure your output like this, as a list of spaces, with spaces separated by newlines, as below. STRICTLY adhere to the formatting, including the serial numbering, with the space number at the beginning of each new space.
1. Age range allowed: <age_range_allowed>. Sex allowed: <sex_allowed>. Cancer primary site allowed: <cancer_primary_site_allowed>. Histology allowed: <histology_allowed>. Disease state allowed: <cancer_burden_allowed>. Prior treatment requirements: <prior_treatment_requirements>. Biomarker requirements: <biomarker_requirements>.
2. Age range allowed: <age_range_allowed>. Sex allowed: <sex_allowed>. ...
If a concept is not relevant, such as if there are no prior treatments required, simply output NA for that concept.
CRITICAL: Anytime you provide a list for a particular concept, you must be completely clear on whether "or" versus "and" logic applies to the list. For example, do not output "EGFR L858R mutant, TP53 mutant"; if both are required, output "EGFR L858R mutant and TP53 mutant". Similarly, do not output "ER+, PR+"; if the patient can have either an ER or a PR positive tumor or one that is positive for both ER and PR, output "ER+ or PR+".
Create separate spaces when they define meaningfully different populations for treatment decision-making, and retain all applicable shared criteria in every space. For example, if a trial allows patients with either (1) EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer or (2) ALK-rearranged non-small cell lung cancer, that should be output as two separate spaces, one for the EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer and one for the ALK-rearranged non-small cell lung cancer, even if all other criteria are the same for both spaces.
NEVER put a newline within a single trial space.
After you output the trial spaces, output a newline, then the text "Boilerplate exclusions:" VERBATIM, then another newline.
Then, list exclusion criteria described in the trial text that are unrelated to the trial space definitions. Such exclusions tend to be common to clinical trials in general. Common such "boilerplate" exclusion criteria include a history of pneumonitis, heart failure, renal dysfunction, liver dysfunction, uncontrolled brain metastases, HIV or hepatitis, prior unrelated cancer diagnoses, and poor performance status. Make sure your boilerplate exclusions are clearly phrased as exclusion criteria, not inclusion criteria. For example, if a trial requires ECOG 0 or 1 for inclusion, do NOT write "ECOG 0 or 1" in the boilerplate exclusions; instead, write "ECOG > 1" or similar language that clearly indicates this is an exclusion criterion. If a trial specifies INCLUSION criteria in a boilerplate category using an 'and' conjunction that patients must meet to enroll on the trial (eg, "ECOG 0 and Hct > 30"), then logically, you should phrase your boilerplate EXCLUSION line using 'or' conjunctions (eg, "ECOG > 0 or Hct < 30" would exclude the patient). Similarly, if an INCLUSION criterion in a boilerplate category is specified with an 'or' conjunction (eg, Hct > 30 or Hgb > 10), then the EXCLUSION criterion you list would be "Hct <= 30 and Hgb <= 10".
ALWAYS output plain text only. NEVER output unicode, Markdown, or tables.
