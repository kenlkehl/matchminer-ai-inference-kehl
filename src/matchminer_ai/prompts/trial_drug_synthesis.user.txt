You will be given Internet search results about a cancer drug. Retain relevant drug-specific facts at every evidence maturity level, where levels include authoritative ontology definitions, preclinical findings, first-in-human or phase 1 observations, preliminary conference or registry results, and mature trial results. Your goal is to synthesize information about the mechanism and targets of the drug; cancer types for which a drug, or its class, has evidence of benefit; and about whether the drug targets a biomarker or other moelcularly-defined paradigm, in which case, you must also synthesize information about the prevalence of that biomarker/molecular paradigm across cancer types, and whether it has evidence of clinical benefit against that biomarker/paradigm. Clearly label the evidence level and distinguish a trial's rationale or design from observed human outcomes. Lack of approval, randomization, publication, mature follow-up, or positive efficacy is not a reason to omit an otherwise supported fact. Preserve tumor/histology/regimen and biomarker forms and preserve prevalence denominators. Do not infer facts not supported by a passage.

Return concise JSON only, consisting of an object with arrays named mechanism_and_targets, efficacy_by_tumor, biomarker_prevalence, biomarker_directed_efficacy, safety, and limitations.

Every item in every array must contain text and support_ids.

`text` is the item. It is one self-contained sentence that an oncologist will read on its own, with no heading, no surrounding items and no access to these passages. A registry passage may support the agent, target, disease, phase, regimen, or study status it explicitly describes, but not an unreported result. Put material immaturity and missing results in limitations rather than silently discarding the underlying supported facts. Use only supplied P# identifiers. An array should be empty only when no supplied passage supports a relevant fact for that category; do not apply a minimum evidence-grade threshold.
