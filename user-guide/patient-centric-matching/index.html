    How matching works¶

    This page explains the ideas behind the matching workflow: what a cohort is, how criteria and patient summaries are divided, what each of the seven steps does, and what the decision model's scores mean. The quickstart shows the code, and the example notebook runs it on sample data.

    MatchMiner-AI ranks and screens candidate pairs for human review. Its scores are not eligibility decisions: every suggested match needs review by a clinician and the trial team against the complete, current protocol.

    Two directions¶

    • Patient-centric: find the cohorts worth considering for a patient.
    • Trial-centric: find the patients worth considering for a cohort.

    Both directions use the same summaries, vectors and checks. Only retrieval changes: it returns the top-k cohorts for each patient, or the top-k patients for each cohort.

    Cohorts¶

    Trials are matched at the level of cohorts. A cohort is one population a trial enrolls with its own eligibility criteria. A single-population trial has one cohort; a basket or umbrella trial can have many.

    • Equivalent cohorts are merged. Extraction first lists every part, phase, arm or group the trial enrolls into. Groups whose criteria are equivalent then become one cohort, and merged_from records the originals. So randomized arms that enroll the same population are one cohort: only the treatment the trial assigns may differ. A required background or concurrent therapy, one the patient must already be receiving, is eligibility, so it keeps cohorts apart.
    • Names never narrow a population. A cohort's name only identifies it. Its population comes from the document's criteria, and features the document gives only as examples ("solid tumors such as...") are not requirements.
    • Groups without their own criteria section are still cohorts, for example parts implied by the numbering or regimens given to separate groups.

    Each cohort has the identifier cohort_trial_id, <trial_id>-cohort-<n>.

    Key and boilerplate criteria¶

    Every line of a cohort's criteria is tagged twice: inclusion or exclusion, and key or boilerplate.

    • Key criteria define the population: cancer type, histology, extent of disease, prior treatment and biomarkers, along with age or sex when the trial restricts them. They are in cohort_key_criteria.
    • Boilerplate criteria are the general requirements most trials share, such as performance status, organ function, brain metastases and other cancers. They are in cohort_boilerplate_criteria.
    • Every line with its tags is in cohort_criteria_items, which the line-by-line check reads.

    Retrieval and the match questions read only the key criteria, so cohorts are compared on the population they target rather than on text that nearly every trial shares.

    Patient summaries¶

    summarize_patients reads each patient's notes in date order. Repeated sentences are removed, the record is split into overlapping chunks, and each chunk updates the running summary written from the earlier ones. There is no note selection or retrieval step: every note is read. The summary has two sections that mirror the criteria:

    • cancer_history_summary, the key section: diagnoses, histology, stage and extent, biomarkers and treatments;
    • general_exclusion_criteria_evidence: the other history that boilerplate criteria ask about.

    patient_summary holds both sections. When new notes arrive, an existing summary can be updated from the new notes only (see patients).

    Step 1 screens each trial for cancer treatment first; trials that are not cancer treatment trials produce no cohorts, and the decisions are kept in the run's metadata. Its parts (extract_cohorts or extract_protocol_cohorts, merge_equivalent_cohorts, remove_boilerplate_criteria) are also public functions, so a caller can run some and skip others; see trials.

    Steps 5-7 are independent checks of the same pairs. A typical review keeps the pairs that pass step 5, then reads steps 6 and 7 as flags.

    The decision model's questions¶

    The decision model, LiquidAI/d1-2B or a checkpoint fine-tuned from it by the training pipeline, answers yes/no questions about a pair in one forward pass. Each answer is reported as P(yes).

    Question Asked by Reads Yes means
    Reasonableness score_match_quality cancer history, key criteria The patient is potentially eligible; no means clearly ineligible
    Cancer type, disease burden, treatment history, biomarkers score_match_quality cancer history, key criteria The cohort specifically requires that dimension and the patient documentedly meets it
    Boilerplate exclusion_criteria_check other history, boilerplate criteria The patient does not clearly fail any boilerplate criterion
    Per criterion check_eligibility_criteria both summary sections, one criteria line The patient does not clearly fail the line; no means an inclusion line clearly not met or an exclusion line clearly met

    "Clearly" matters: missing information is not a failure. A patient whose record says nothing about a criterion is left for the reviewer, not screened out.

    Scores and cutoffs¶

    • match_quality_score ranks pairs from 0 to 1. It is the expected match score, P(reasonable) x (1 + P(cancer type) + P(burden) + P(treatment history) + 2 P(biomarker)) / 6, so a pair ranks higher when the patient is potentially eligible and the cohort is specific about the dimensions the patient matches.
    • match_quality_pass is true when P(reasonable) is at least match_quality.reasonableness_cutoff (0.5). It is the model's own potentially-eligible call; the score is for ranking only.
    • exclusion_criteria_pass is true when the boilerplate P(yes) is at least exclusion_criteria.pass_cutoff (0.5).
    • criterion_clearly_fails is true when a line's P(yes) is below criteria_check.fail_cutoff (0.5).

    score_match_quality returns only passing pairs unless filter_low_quality=False, and check_eligibility_criteria can return only failing lines (filter_failing=True). Outputs are derived tables keyed on patient_id and cohort_trial_id; merge them back onto the pairs.

    The default decision model is the zero-shot base model. Treat its scores as provisional until it is replaced by a fine-tuned checkpoint (decision_model.model_name) evaluated for your setting.

    LLM versions of the checks¶

    score_match_quality_with_llm, exclusion_criteria_check_with_llm and check_eligibility_criteria_with_llm ask an LLM the same questions and return a reason with each answer. The match and per-criterion versions use the prompts the training pipeline's teacher uses to label pairs. They are slower and send both texts to the configured endpoint, so use them where a written reason helps the reviewer and the endpoint is approved for the data.

    Models¶

    • LLM. Steps 1 and 2 use nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL-120B-A12B-NVFP4 by default, in-process with vLLM or on an OpenAI-compatible server. See choosing an inference setup and LLM runtime.
    • TrialSpace. A sentence-embedding model fine-tuned from google/embeddinggemma-2 by the training pipeline. The default preset names none, so set embedding.model_path to your own. Vectors from different TrialSpace models are not comparable: re-embed both sides after changing the model.
    • Decision model. Runs in-process on a GPU with Transformers.

    The published models and synthetic datasets are on Hugging Face. The latest release, collection 0526, comes from the earlier trial-space pipeline: its ModernBERT checkers are not used by this package, and its TrialSpace model was trained on a different text format than cohort key criteria.

    Guideline populations¶

    The same retrieval and match questions can run against guideline catalogs instead of trials. A catalog built from NCCN or NCI PDQ guidelines describes populations ("spaces", identified by space_trial_id) with cohort-style key criteria, and matching.retrieve_guideline_considerations finds the ones that fit a patient. See guideline extraction and PDQ catalogs.
